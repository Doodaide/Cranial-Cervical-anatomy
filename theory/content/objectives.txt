# Regional learning objectives, Doctor of Dental Medicine head and neck anatomy (full year)
#
# This file is the checklist the theory pages are measured against. Do not renumber by hand: item ids
# (skull.o1, skull.s7.2, ...) are made from the order here, and each theory section lists the ids it covers.
# Run  python3 tools/build_theory.py --ids  to print every id with its coverage.
#
# Format
#   # <slug> | <number> | <region title>        starts a region (the slug is the theory page name)
#   ## objectives | structures | clinical       starts a list (regional objectives; regions and structures; clinical relevance)
#   - item                                      an item; two spaces of indent make it a sub-item
#   - item || note                              a note shown under the item on the checklist page
#
# Wording is the department's, with spelling slips corrected. Three corrections change a word, not just a spelling:
#   Neck, objective 1: "facial arrangement" -> "fascial arrangement" (the structures listed are fasciae)
#   Oral cavity, objective 1: "muscles immediately the oral cavity" -> "muscles immediately around the oral cavity"
#   Cranial cavity: the two arteries and circle of Willis are nested under "blood supply" (the source flattens them)
#   Ear: the source nests External ear, Middle ear and Inner ear under blank bullets; they are top-level items here, and "Cochlear" is written "Cochlea"
#   Nose: the source title "Anatomy Nose, Maxilla and V2" is shortened to "Nose, maxilla and V2"

# skull | 1 | Skull and vertebral column
## objectives
- Identify individual bones which make up the skull
- To understand the articulations of the bones of the skull
- Have an understanding of the development of the maxilla and mandible
- Begin to identify structures, foramina and structures traversing them, on the face and base of skull, with particular attention to the mandible and maxilla
- To understand the layout of the vertebral column, with specific reference to the cervical region
## structures
- Location of individual bones and articulations:
  - frontal, temporal, parietal, occipital, ethmoid, sphenoid
  - maxilla, zygoma, lacrimal, nasal, inferior concha, palatine, vomer, mandible
- Mandible in detail
- Maxilla in detail
- Major features and foramina of external skull and facial skeleton:
  - see workshop notes for list || The course list is in workshop notes, which this site does not use as a source. These foramina are covered from Last's and Cunningham's instead, so the lists may differ.
- Major features and foramina of skull base:
  - see workshop notes for list || The course list is in workshop notes, which this site does not use as a source. These foramina are covered from Last's and Cunningham's instead, so the lists may differ.
- Features of typical and atypical cervical vertebra (C1, C2):
  - articulations and shape of articular facets
  - anterior arch of C1 and odontoid process of C2
  - foramen transversarium and vertebral artery

# thorax | 2 | Thorax
## objectives
- Describe main anatomical features of trachea and lungs, including understanding of location of inhaled foreign bodies
- Describe the divisions of the mediastinum with regards to spread of oro-facial infection
- Understand pleura and root of lung
- Identify major anatomical features of heart - chambers, valves
- Describe coronary circulation
- Describe course of ascending aorta and descending thoracic aorta. Name major branches
- Locate sympathetic trunk and understand its significance
- Identify course and distribution of vagus and phrenic nerves
## structures
- Layout of thoracic cavity:
  - diaphragm
  - aperture of thorax (inlet/outlet)
  - pleural cavities
  - mediastinum
  - chest wall - major features including intercostal space
- Mediastinum - anterior, superior, middle, posterior:
  - boundaries
  - contents
- Trachea - anatomy and relations
- Left and right primary bronchi - arrangement and relations
- Diaphragm - function and innervation
- Phrenic nerve - roots, distribution and course
- Vagus nerve within the thorax
- Pleura - parietal and visceral
- Pericardium
- Heart - base, apex:
  - chambers
  - valves - aortic, pulmonary, atrio-ventricular (bicuspid and tricuspid)
  - coronary circulation
  - innervation
  - conduction system
  - fetal circulation, and congenital defects
- Great vessels around root of the neck
- Rib 1 and relations
- Sympathetic trunk
- Azygos venous system
- Thoracic duct
- Oesophagus

# neck | 3 | Neck
## objectives
- To understand the fascial arrangement of the neck, including structures related to each layer
- Identify and understand the bony and cartilaginous components of the neck
- Describe the anatomy and embryology of the thyroid gland and position of embryological remnants
- Describe and understand the contents of the triangles of the neck especially the anterior triangle
- Describe the cervical plexus - cutaneous branches
- Describe the position of the carotid sheath and the anatomy of its contents
- Identify ansa cervicalis, the supra- and infra-hyoid muscles and their specific innervations
- Describe the position and arrangement of lymph nodes in the neck
## structures
- Superficial fascia, platysma and branches of the cervical plexus
- External and anterior jugular veins
- Investing layer of deep cervical fascia
- Sternocleidomastoid muscle
- Trapezius muscle
- Boundaries and contents of the triangles of the neck
- Accessory nerve
- Ansa cervicalis
- Infra- and supra-hyoid muscles
- Superficial part of the submandibular salivary gland
- Anterior and posterior bellies of digastric
- Position of hypoglossal nerve in the neck
- Carotid sheath and relations:
  - internal jugular vein
  - vagus nerve
  - common carotid artery
- Branches of the external carotid artery in neck
- Thyroid cartilage
- Cricoid cartilage
- Trachea
- Thyroid gland - blood supply and relations
- Position of oesophagus
- Pre-vertebral fascia and related structures:
  - scalenus anterior
  - phrenic nerve
  - vertebral artery

# face | 4 | Face and parotid
## objectives
- Describe the sensory innervation of regions of the face
- Describe the extra cranial course of the facial nerve and its branches and relations
- Identify muscles of the face controlled by the facial nerve - their arrangement and relations
- Describe the blood supply and venous drainage of the face and locations of these vessels
- Describe the location of the parotid gland and related anatomy
- Describe the lymphatic drainage of the face
## structures
- Sensory innervation of face in detail:
  - cutaneous branches of trigeminal nerve - ophthalmic, maxillary and mandibular distributions
  - branches of the cervical plexus
- Motor innervation of muscles of the face:
  - facial nerve - extra cranial course, distribution and branches within the parotid gland and on the face
  - position of marginal mandibular branch of facial
- Musculature of face - sphincters and dilators of orifices:
  - position of modiolus
  - levators and depressors of the mouth
  - buccinator in detail
  - bony attachments to mandible and maxilla
  - orbicularis oculi
  - occipito-frontalis
- Parotid gland:
  - location: parotid "bed" or parotid fossa
  - relationship of external carotid artery, retro-mandibular vein and facial nerve within the gland
  - presence of accessory parotid gland
  - location of parotid duct
- Facial artery:
  - origin, relationship with submandibular salivary gland
  - course on the face including superior and inferior labial arteries
  - anastomosis with ophthalmic artery
- Transverse facial artery
- Facial vein:
  - drainage and communication with other veins - ophthalmic and deep facial vein

# tmj | 5 | TMJ, temporalis, masseter
## objectives
- To be able to describe the anatomy of the temporomandibular joint complex - static and in function:
  - position, structures involved, movements, vascular supply and innervation
- Able to describe attachments, morphology, nerve and blood supply, and actions of:
  - masseter muscle
  - lateral pterygoid muscle
  - temporalis muscle
## structures
- Mandible
- Sphenoid bone
- Zygomatic bone
- Temporal bone
- Temporal fossa - boundaries
- Temporalis muscle - attachments, actions
- Temporomandibular joint:
  - lateral ligament of the TMJ
  - capsule
  - articular disc
  - upper and lower joint spaces
  - innervation and blood supply
  - functional anatomy - actions, attachments of lateral pterygoid muscle
- Auriculotemporal nerve and superficial temporal artery
- Temporalis fascia and temporo-parietal fascia
- Temporal branch of the facial nerve
- Deep temporal nerves and deep temporal arteries
- Masseter muscle - superficial, intermediate and deep heads, actions, blood supply and nerve supply
- Lateral pterygoid muscle - attachments and actions of superior and inferior heads
- Long buccal nerve and artery
- Zygomaticofacial nerve (V2)
## clinical
- Mastication and function of muscles acting upon the joint
- Temporomandibular joint disorders including myalgia
- Dislocation of mandible and how to reduce
- Trauma - condylar fracture, zygomatic arch fracture

# itf | 6 | Infratemporal fossa, V3
## objectives
- To be able to describe the boundaries, contents and relationships of structures within the infratemporal fossa
- To dissect and display the structures at the mandibular foramen
- Understand the mandibular division of the trigeminal nerve in its entirety
- Appreciate the variation in structures within the population
## structures
- Sphenoid bone
- Temporal bone
- Infratemporal fossa - location, boundaries and communications
- Boundaries of the pterygomandibular space
- Maxillary artery - 1st and 2nd parts, including:
  - middle meningeal, accessory meningeal
  - inferior alveolar
- All branches of the mandibular division of trigeminal nerve, with special emphasis on:
  - inferior alveolar
  - lingual
  - long buccal
  - auriculotemporal
- Motor root of trigeminal
- Otic ganglion
- Chorda tympani nerve
- Lateral pterygoid muscle - attachments, actions:
  - superior and inferior heads
- Medial pterygoid muscle - attachments, actions
- Sphenomandibular ligament
- Pterygoid venous plexus and communications
- Deep lobe of the parotid gland
- Buccinator attachments
- Buccal fat pad
## clinical
- Anaesthesia of the mandibular division of trigeminal nerve
- Trismus
- Haematoma
- Infections and possible spread to adjacent fascial spaces
- Foreign bodies

# cranial | 7 | Cranial cavity and brain
## objectives
- Review features and foramina of cranial cavity
- Describe parts and gross features of the brain
- Identify all cranial nerves on the surface of the brain
- Understand blood supply and venous drainage of the brain
- Understand the arrangement of dura and dural venous sinuses
- Describe the communications and contents of the cavernous venous sinus
## structures
- Cranial cavity - features and foramina
- Sphenoid bone
- Frontal bone
- Ethmoid bone
- Temporal bone
- Cranial cavity:
  - Anterior and middle cranial fossae
  - Attachments of dura mater
  - Dural venous sinuses and venous drainage
  - general drainage pathways
  - communications to outside of cranial cavity including infratemporal fossa and face
  - Cavernous sinus
  - Course of the middle meningeal artery
- Brain:
  - dura mater
  - hemispheres, general layout
  - location of somatosensory cortex
  - post- and pre-central gyri
  - blood supply: internal carotid, vertebral artery; circle of Willis
  - cranial nerves 1-12
## clinical
- Cranial nerve assessment
- Trauma: including extradural haemorrhage, subarachnoid haemorrhage
- Pathology and cavernous sinus thrombosis

# orbit | 8 | Orbit, V1
## objectives
- Understand and describe distribution of ophthalmic division of trigeminal nerve
- Understand and describe distribution of cranial nerves I, II, III, IV and VI
- Recount the bones of the orbit and describe the orbital walls
- Describe the actions of the muscles acting upon the globe
- Understand and describe position, function and innervation of the lacrimal gland
## structures
- Bones which make up the orbit
- Anterior cranial fossa
- Cavernous sinus
- Oculomotor nerve
- Trochlear nerve
- Ophthalmic division of trigeminal nerve and distributions:
  - Nasociliary nerve
  - Frontal
  - Lacrimal
- Abducent nerve
- Optic nerve
- Internal carotid artery
- Ophthalmic artery
- Ophthalmic veins and communications to face
- Ciliary ganglion, and long and short ciliary nerves
- Sympathetic and parasympathetic innervation to structures within the orbit
- Lacrimal gland - location and innervation
- Nasolacrimal duct
- Muscles acting on the globe, their actions and innervation:
  - Recti - lateral, medial, superior, inferior
  - Superior and inferior oblique muscles
- Levator palpebrae superioris muscle - action and innervation
- General anatomy of the globe
- Pupillary and ciliary muscles and actions
## clinical
- Infections - bacterial and viral
- Trauma
- Cavernous sinus thrombosis
- Horner's syndrome

# oral | 9 | Oral cavity
## objectives
- Describe the muscles immediately around the oral cavity and their actions
- Understand the anatomy of the floor of the mouth in detail
- Describe the tongue, innervations, blood supply
- Describe the innervation, blood supply and lymphatic drainage of structures of the oral cavity
## structures
- Mandible
- Hyoid bone
- Sensory innervation to mucosa of the oral cavity
- Buccinator muscle
- Pterygomandibular raphe
- Attachments of superior pharyngeal constrictor
- Mental foramen, nerve and artery
- Anatomy of tongue:
  - intrinsic and extrinsic muscles
  - styloglossus
  - palatoglossus
  - blood and nerve supply in detail
  - lymphatic drainage - tip of tongue vs elsewhere
- Mylohyoid muscle
- Anterior belly of digastric
- Genioglossus and geniohyoid muscles
- Hyoglossus muscle
- Long buccal nerve
- Lingual nerve
- Submandibular salivary duct
- Sublingual veins
- Hypoglossal nerve
- Submandibular space
- Submandibular gland - deep and superficial parts
- Location of sublingual gland
- Parotid duct opening
## clinical
- All dental procedures including diagnosis
- Anatomical risks from various surgical procedures such as:
  - tooth removal, mandibular third molar removal
  - floor of mouth surgery (e.g. frenectomy, sialolith removal)
  - mandibular osteotomies

# pharynx | 10 | Pharynx
## objectives
- Describe the regions of the pharynx, including their main features
- Describe the regional innervation of the mucosa of the pharynx
- Describe the arrangement of the musculature of the pharynx and actions of the constrictors
- Understand lymphatic drainage of the pharynx
- Understand and describe the anatomical mechanism of swallowing - phases, muscles, nerves involved
- Understand the immediate adjacent anatomy of the pharynx, including retropharyngeal space
## structures
- Skull:
  - Pharyngeal tubercle
  - Medial pterygoid plate and pterygoid hamulus
  - Groove for pharyngotympanic tube
- Median raphe
- Fascia:
  - Pharyngobasilar
  - Buccopharyngeal
- Hyoid bone and stylohyoid ligament
- Cartilages:
  - Thyroid
  - Cricoid
  - Epiglottis
- Thyrohyoid membrane
- Constrictors:
  - Circular - superior, middle, inferior
  - Vertical - stylopharyngeus, palatopharyngeus, salpingopharyngeus
- Pharyngeal recess
- Piriform recess
- Epiglottic valleculae and median and lateral glosso-epiglottic folds
- Oesophagus
- Glossopharyngeal nerve, vagus nerve
- Superior cervical ganglion
- Carotid sheath
## clinical
- Foreign body lodgement
- Swallowing, dysphagia
- Pharyngeal diverticula
- Eagle's syndrome
- Tonsil disease and tonsilloliths

# larynx | 11 | Larynx and tongue
## objectives
- Understand and describe the anatomy of the tongue and floor of mouth:
  - Path of lingual nerve and relations
  - Submandibular salivary duct
  - Intrinsic and extrinsic muscles of the tongue
  - Blood supply, venous drainage and lymphatics
  - Innervation - sensory and motor
- Understand and describe the anatomy of the larynx:
  - Hard tissue framework
  - Musculature of the larynx
  - Innervation, blood supply and lymphatics
## structures
- Lingual nerve, submandibular salivary gland and duct
- Sublingual veins
- Hypoglossal nerve
- Mylohyoid, genioglossus and geniohyoid muscles
- Palatoglossus and palatopharyngeus muscles
- Lingual and facial arteries
- Epiglottis and epiglottic valleculae
- Hyoid bone and thyrohyoid membrane
- Thyroid, cricoid and arytenoid cartilages
- Superior laryngeal nerve
- Recurrent laryngeal nerve
- False and true vocal folds
- Ventricle of the larynx
## clinical
- Speech
- Swallowing
- Foreign body - choking
- Mandibular third molar surgery
- Lymphatic draining of the tongue
- Sialoliths and blocked salivary ducts (sialadenitis)
- Intubation
- Pathology of the tongue and floor of the mouth

# nose | 12 | Nose, maxilla and V2
## objectives
- Understand the bony arrangement of the mid-face specifically the maxilla, nasal cavity and palate
- Describe the arrangement of the paranasal sinuses and the locations of their openings
- Describe the anatomy of the palate and its musculature
- Understand the blood supply, nerve supply and lymphatic drainage of the nose and palate
- Describe the contents of the pterygopalatine fossa and the distribution of the maxillary nerve
## structures
- Bones and features:
  - maxilla, palatine, ethmoid, vomer, sphenoid, inferior concha
  - pterygoid canal, foramen rotundum
  - Nasal septum
  - Nasal conchae
  - Superior, middle, and inferior meatus
- Pterygopalatine fossa:
  - 3rd part of the maxillary artery and tributaries
  - maxillary nerve V2, and distribution
  - pterygopalatine ganglion
- Hiatus semilunaris and bulla ethmoidalis
- Maxillary sinus and paranasal sinuses
- Palatal musculature:
  - Palatoglossus
  - Palatopharyngeus
  - Levator veli palatini
  - Tensor veli palatini
  - Musculus uvulae
## clinical
- Smell and nasal function
- Speech
- Swallowing
- Trauma
- Nasal intubation
- Maxillary tooth removal and minor oral surgery
- Pathology of the palate and maxilla

# ear | 13 | Ear and temporal bone
## objectives
- Describe the main features of the temporal bone
- Understand the anatomy of the external ear:
  - cartilaginous and bony components
  - external acoustic meatus
  - tympanic membrane & its features
  - Innervation
- Understand the functional anatomy of the middle ear:
  - tympanic cavity - features and boundaries
  - ossicles and sound wave transmission; associated muscles
  - pharyngotympanic tube and mastoid
- Understand the functional anatomy of the inner ear:
  - Inner ear - bony & membranous labyrinth
  - cochlea, semicircular canals
- Describe the contribution of facial, glossopharyngeal and vagus nerves
- Describe the facial nerve in detail
- Understand the preganglionics related to the temporal bone
## structures
- Temporal bone:
  - squamous, petromastoid, tympanic and styloid portions
  - processes and foramina
- External ear:
  - features
  - innervation: cervical plexus, great auricular, auriculotemporal, facial, vagus
- Middle ear:
  - ossicles
  - epitympanic recess, aditus to the tympanic antrum
  - the antrum and mastoid air cells
  - pharyngotympanic tube
  - promontory
  - round and oval windows on the medial wall of the middle ear
  - tensor tympani and stapedius muscles & their innervations
- Inner ear:
  - Semicircular canals
  - Cochlea
  - Vestibulocochlear nerve
- Geniculate ganglion
- Nervus intermedius
- Greater superficial petrosal nerve
- Lesser superficial petrosal nerve
- Chorda tympani nerve
## clinical
- 1st and 2nd pharyngeal arch syndromes
- Viral infections, including HSV and Ramsay-Hunt syndrome
- Hearing and balance
