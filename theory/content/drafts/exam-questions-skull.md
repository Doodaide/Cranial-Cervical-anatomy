# Draft examination questions, region 1 (not used by the build; kept for the examination page)

:::mcq
Which statement about the vertebral artery in the neck is correct?

A. It usually enters the foramen transversarium of C7.
B. It usually enters the foramen transversarium of C6 and ascends through the foramina as far as the atlas.
C. After leaving the foramen of the atlas it passes in front of the lateral mass to reach the foramen magnum.
D. Within the foramina transversaria it lies behind the emerging spinal nerves.
E. Between the axis and the atlas it runs straight up without any lateral deviation.

Answer: B

Explanation: The artery arises from the first part of the subclavian artery and normally disappears into the foramen of the C6 transverse process, then climbs through the foramina of the upper six cervical vertebrae. That is also why C1 to C6 are the foramina that transmit it. The foramen of C7 does not carry the artery.

Option review:
A: Incorrect. The C7 foramen is small and carries veins only. The artery usually enters at C6 and only rarely at a higher level.
B: Correct. This is the usual course of the first and second parts.
C: Incorrect. After the atlas foramen the artery curves backwards and medially behind the lateral mass, then grooves the posterior arch before reaching the foramen magnum.
D: Incorrect. In the second part the artery ascends in front of the emerging spinal nerves.
E: Incorrect. Between the axis and atlas it passes laterally with a pronounced backward convexity and then loops upwards beside the atlantoaxial joint, which provides slack for rotation of the atlas.

Sources: last Ch. 6, Part 4 (Root of the neck), and Part 18 (Suboccipital triangle); cunn ch. 2, p. 7

Which structure holds the dens of the axis against the anterior arch of the atlas and prevents its backward displacement into the vertebral canal?

A. The apical ligament
B. The alar ligaments
C. The transverse ligament of the atlas
D. The anterior longitudinal ligament
E. The ligamenta flava

Answer: C

Explanation: The transverse ligament is a strong band that runs behind the dens, between tubercles on the two lateral masses of the atlas. It grooves the back of the dens and holds it against the anterior arch. If it and the tectorial membrane ruptured, the dens could dislocate backwards and compress the medulla.

Option review:
A: Incorrect. The apical ligament is weak and runs from the tip of the dens to the front of the foramen magnum. It is a notochord remnant and not a restraint.
B: Incorrect. The alar ligaments run from the sides of the dens to the margins of the foramen magnum and limit rotation of the head.
C: Correct. It is the transverse band of the cruciform ligament and lies directly behind the dens.
D: Incorrect. This ligament runs down the front of the vertebral bodies, from the basiocciput and the anterior tubercle of the atlas to the sacrum. It is not behind the dens.
E: Incorrect. The ligamenta flava join the laminae of adjacent vertebrae and are stretched by flexion. They do not hold the dens.

Sources: last Ch. 6, Part 18 (Vertebral joints, and Special vertebrae and joints); cunn ch. 2, p. 7
